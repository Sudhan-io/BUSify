Problem:
Many students at the college pay bus fees but still end up traveling standing, because bus allocation across routes doesn't match actual student demand at each stop. Some buses run under-filled while others are overcrowded, and the current system doesn't adjust to how many students are actually waiting at each stop or area at a given time. This is a direct access and equity failure — students are paying for a service that isn't reliably delivering what it promises (SDG 11: Sustainable Cities & Communities, specifically the target on safe, accessible, affordable transport for all; it also touches SDG 10: Reduced Inequalities, since it hits hardest for students with no alternative way to commute).
Solution:
A smart, demand-aware bus allocation system that replaces the current fixed/static bus assignment with one that actually responds to where students are and how many there are.
Predict demand — Estimate how many students will need pickup from each stop and area at different times of day, instead of assuming the same fixed numbers every day.
Allocate the fleet intelligently — Given a fixed number of buses (e.g. 50), decide how many should be sent to each area based on that predicted demand, so buses go where the crowding actually is rather than following a rigid, unchanging schedule.
Plan efficient routes within each area — Once an area has its buses, work out the best order to visit its stops so every student is picked up while keeping the total distance (and fuel use) as low as possible.
Make it visible — Give students a way to see which buses/times are likely to be crowded so they can plan around it, and give the college transport office a way to see where reallocating buses would help most

For this project, I’d lock the tech stack to this. **Don’t over-stack it.** The goal is a working system first, sophisticated infrastructure second.

## Data schema
- `stops(stop_id, name, lat, lon, area_id)`
- `buses(bus_id, seats, area_id)`
- `registrations(student_hash, stop_id)`, with student IDs hashed for privacy
- `calendar(date, is_holiday, exam_flag, first_class_time)`
- `demand_obs(stop_id, date, slot, riders, source)`, where `source` is one of attendance, survey, or field count
- `plans(date, slot, bus_id, area_id, route_json)`

## Workflow
1. **Ingest.** Load the transport office's Excel or CSV into PostGIS after cleaning and anonymizing.
2. **Feature build.** Compute expected riders per area, slot, and date from registrations × attendance rate.
3. **Forecast.** Retrain nightly or weekly, then predict the next 7 days at p50 and p90.
4. **Allocate.** Solve the fleet split per slot.
5. **Route.** Solve one capacitated routing problem per area.
6. **Serve.** Expose the plans and crowd scores through the API.
7. **Evaluate.** Replay historical days in the simulator and compare against the current allocation.

## Model design decisions
- **Forecast at area-slot level, not stop level.** Per-stop data will be too sparse. Split the area forecast to stops in proportion to registrations.
- **Baseline first.** Same-weekday average of recent weeks. LightGBM must beat it on WAPE, or the ML is not justified.
- **Features:** weekday, week of semester, exam/holiday flag, first-class time, rolling 7 and 14 day means, lagged counts.
- **Fallback if ground truth is thin:** attendance-based demand from your college's attendance records, calibrated on your field counts.

## Allocation formulation
- **Variables:** integer `n_a` (buses to area `a`), slack `s_a` (standing students), `e_a` (empty seats), and `d_a` (change from the current allocation).
- **Constraints:**
  - `Σ n_a = 50`
  - `n_a ≥ n_min` (coverage)
  - `s_a ≥ D_a^p90 − seats × n_a`
  - `d_a ≥ |n_a − n_current,a|`
- **Objective:** minimize `Σ s_a + λ Σ e_a + μ Σ d_a`. The `μ` term makes the plan propose small moves instead of a full reshuffle.

## Routing
- Capacity dimension = seats. Time dimension = arrive before the first class starts.
- Each area is solved independently, with the depot at the college.
- The output is an ordered stop list, pickup times, and total km per bus.

## API endpoints
- `GET /forecast?date=&slot=`: demand p50 and p90 by area
- `GET /plan?date=&slot=`: bus counts per area and routes
- `GET /crowding?bus_id=&date=`: crowd level (low, medium, high) for the student view
- `POST /simulate`: run status quo vs proposed on a date range
